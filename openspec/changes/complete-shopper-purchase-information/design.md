Launch task 2.7 owns selling-information acceptance; tasks 2.8 and 2.9 own tax/fiscal acceptance and operations. This child owns the public content and its placement, supplying evidence back to 2.7.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:** Make approved answers accessible before purchase without introducing a second pricing policy or duplicating long legal prose across screens.

The accepted Store Item design direction also groups identity, exact option, price, and purchase action. The reviewed Disintegration mobile page placed price/Add to Cart after the large image and full description, repeated the release title in a nested price panel, and displayed release-wide formats ahead of the actual Black Vinyl LP option.

**Non-Goals:** Invented legal terms, changing returns policy, a consent-management platform, new shipping modes, new tax calculations, fiscal-provider integration, or legal approval through test results.

## Decisions

1. **One reviewed content source.** Use the existing EmDash `purchase_information` singleton and shared content-model schema, with seller/support fields, dispatch/locker summaries, terms sections, privacy sections, and revision date. The public runtime reads only its accepted snapshot; repository JSON is retained fixture/bootstrap input, not the live editing path. Keep long approved wording in that entry and render excerpts from its explicit summary fields; do not derive promises through truncation or duplicate them in component literals. No generic legal-document framework.
2. **Keep existing URLs useful.** `/terms/` supplies purchase and delivery information with stable anchors for delivery, returns/refunds, damaged items, and uncollected parcels. `/privacy/` is exposed only for approved content. Both remain directly accessible server-rendered documents with appropriate titles, canonical metadata, sitemap inclusion, and retained static-build support. Do not add them to same-document shell routing in this slice.
3. **Put information at the decision.** Store Item pages use one coherent group ordered title, artist, exact sellable option, current price, and Add to Cart, with concise approved dispatch/locker information and terms/support links adjacent. Put the long editorial description below this group; do not repeat the release title in a nested price panel. On mobile, place the group before the large artwork/gallery and long copy so those do not push the buying decision down the page. Desktop artwork may remain beside it. Make Back to Store a quieter text link with a usable target and visible focus. Other release formats belong in clearly editorial context, not the purchase-option label. Preserve current price/availability loading, unavailable, and disabled-action states; a static Available badge must not override runtime readiness. No sticky duplicate purchase bar is required. Cart and checkout show concise delivery/returns/help links beside existing summaries. Footer links expose both terms and privacy. Forms collecting personal data expose privacy nearby; preserve existing newsletter consent and avoid adding a generic mandatory checkout checkbox without an approved requirement.
4. **Preserve monetary authority.** Reuse `DeliveryRates` and the existing VAT/delivery summary components. Rates and totals are not authored in the new content record. While policy reads are unavailable, show the current unavailable state rather than an invented free-delivery promise. Accepted historical totals remain tied to their order snapshot.
5. **Review facts before publication.** During implementation, inventory current account-confirmed public seller details and request only missing approved wording. Track evidence by field/section in a change-local checklist. Required inputs are public seller identity/address and contact, dispatch timing, locker arrangements, return/refund/defect/uncollected handling, and privacy purposes/recipients/retention/rights/contact wording reflecting actual services. Keep private account records outside Git. Publish only the approved public text; no invented deadlines or legal conclusions.
6. **Avoid overlapping ownership.** This change is the single implementation owner for public purchase/privacy content, its placement, and the Store Item purchase hierarchy; launch task 2.7 and VAT task 4.4 consume its evidence. The VAT child supplies seller and monetary-policy inputs, but its archival is not a prerequisite for this implementation or publication evidence; the parent accepts both before launch. The listening change adds a secondary Listen action near item identity and verified editorial links without adding another purchase-summary block. Store-wide search owns the compact All collection layout, and Distro enrichment owns images/copy. These feature changes can ship independently; when they touch the same template, integrate with its current markup and reuse applicable presentation evidence rather than replacing each other's sections. Structural hierarchy work may proceed using current factual copy while missing policy wording continues to block publication acceptance.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Missing approved wording delays completion → build and test with synthetic fixtures, keep production publication and launch acceptance pending until required public text is approved.
- Rates or fulfillment promises can drift → keep rates runtime-policy-owned and one versioned editorial source for business commitments.
- Dense copy can obscure purchase actions → use brief summaries and descriptive links, with full static documents available by keyboard and without JavaScript.
- Privacy text can misdescribe providers → inventory actual checkout, newsletter, inquiry, hosting, and optional music-embed data flows before review; this change does not assert compliance by itself.
